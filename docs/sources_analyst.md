# Free sources of analyst estimate data — the search of 7 October 2026

Task A of the free-analyst-data order (7 October 2026). Owner decision: no purchase; find free sources first.

**Method and limits.** The search ran on 7 October 2026 within the two-hour limit (about 45 minutes of fetching across three parallel research passes). No account was created, no API key requested, no trial started, and the owner's email address was used nowhere. Every host's `robots.txt` was read before any request; hosts that disallow automated or AI-agent access were not fetched (Yahoo Finance's own pages, MarketWatch, Seeking Alpha, Macrotrends, the Nasdaq API host, and the SEC, whose fair-access policy requires a declared contact). At most one request per second per host. Every response was cached (about 165 files; the cache lives outside the repository, with an inventory in the research notes). No login or paywall was bypassed; where a site served a bot challenge (Zacks, TipRanks, Kaggle) the challenge was not retried.

Facts are marked **[verified]** when read from a response fetched during the search, and **[per documentation]** when read from a vendor's documentation or a search result only.

**Definition of a usable history of estimate revisions (A3).** A source counts only if all hold: dated consensus EPS for the current fiscal year or next quarter; at least 300 S&P 500 members; at least 12 monthly observations per company; each value carries the date on which it was the consensus (restated values do not count).

## Does any free source meet the definition in A3?

**No.** Every free source is one of: a current snapshot (Nasdaq, Alpha Vantage, FMP, Zacks, MarketWatch, Seeking Alpha, TipRanks, stockanalysis.com, MarketBeat, Yahoo), a per-quarter pre-report estimate (Alpha Vantage `EARNINGS`, the Kaggle and Hugging Face surprise files, Finnhub's four quarters), or a dated panel too sparse in dates or names (the Wayback captures; the two Hugging Face Yahoo scrapes; the consensus-drift file). The sources that do meet the definition are licensed: **I/B/E/S or Zacks through WRDS** (a university login), the terminal and workstation feeds (Bloomberg, LSEG Workspace, FactSet, Capital IQ), **Estimize Premium Plus**, and Nasdaq Data Link's **ZEEH**.

Two free sources are still worth keeping: the **Wayback captures** as a sparse point-in-time validator of the repository's own snapshots (dozens of dated observations for the largest names, a handful for the median S&P name), and **SEC 8-K guidance** as a dated company-guidance series if the owner wants that variable later. Rating changes, price targets and earnings surprises already have free history in the price provider (task B).
