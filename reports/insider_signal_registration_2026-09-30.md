## A. Provenance of the hypothesis

The published prior is Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski (2012), "Decoding Inside Information", *Journal of
Finance* 67(3), 1009–1043: insider trades split into *routine* (the same calendar month in each of the
three prior years) and *opportunistic* (all others) carry very different information — the
opportunistic trades predict future returns and the routine ones do not. The cluster form (several
distinct insiders buying inside a short window) is the operational version this system displays, and
it is the one being registered: a pass on the union universe is evidence that the published effect
survives in this universe, in this period, at these costs, after the neutralizations below — not a
confirmation of the published magnitude. The hypothesis was not generated by looking at this
system's own data; the module that displays the flag (`scripts/ownership/insiders.py`) went live on
the same day as this registration and no return has been computed from it.

## B. The candidate, exactly as the display computes it

* **Open-market trade:** a Form 4 nonDerivative transaction with code P (purchase) or S (sale). Codes
  A, F, M, G, C, D, J, W and every derivative-table row are ignored.
* **Classification (Cohen–Malloy–Pomorski):** for a trade in calendar month *m* of year *y*, the
  insider is *routine* if the same insider (reporting-owner CIK) made an open-market trade at the same
  issuer in month *m* of each of *y−1*, *y−2* and *y−3*; otherwise *opportunistic*. An insider with
  fewer than three prior calendar years of trading history at the issuer is opportunistic by the rule
  (the paper excludes such insiders from classification; this registration keeps them, as the display
  does, and reports the result with them excluded as a sensitivity — see F).
* **Attribution:** a filing with several reporting owners is one trade attributed to the first-listed
  owner; distinct insiders are distinct reporting-owner CIKs.
* **Opportunistic-purchase cluster:** three or more distinct opportunistic insiders with purchase
  dates spanning at most 30 calendar days (last minus first ≤ 30). **Opportunistic-sales cluster:**
  the same test on opportunistic sales.
* **Point-in-time rule (the one thing the display does not need and the test must have):** a purchase
  is known only from its **filing date**, never its transaction date. A cluster is *complete* on the
  filing date of the purchase that makes it three distinct buyers. Classification of each trade uses
  only filings dated on or before that day.

## C. Test universe and data

* **Universe:** the full union universe, `data/universe.txt` (537 names on 30 September 2026), never
  the book. The universe is the present one; names enter the test on their first session with a
  price in the canonical store and leave on their last. The survivorship this implies is a stated
  limitation of the run, reported with it, not a reason to widen the universe after the fact.
* **Insider history:** the SEC quarterly Insider Transactions data sets extracted into
  `data/ownership/insider_history.parquet` by `scripts/ownership/insiders.py` (the same extractor,
  same open-market filter, same classification code as the display), extended back to cover every
  quarter from **2013q1** through the last posted quarter before the run. Because classification needs
  three prior years, the first formation date is the last session of **January 2016**.
* **Prices:** the canonical daily close store (`data/source/prices_daily.parquet`), dividend-adjusted,
  the same series every other retirement test uses. **Window:** from the first formation date to the
  last session of the month before the run, fixed in the results report before the run starts and
  asserted by the script.
* **Cash rate:** the effective federal funds rate as the C3 engine uses it (`fred_indicators.parquet`
  column `effr`, forward-filled, rate/252/100 per session).

## D. Construction (fixed now)

* **Rebalance calendar:** monthly, positions set at the close of the first session of each calendar
  month and held to the next rebalance (the C3 calendar, B4).
* **Information timing:** everything uses filings and prices through the close of the session *before*
  the rebalance session (t−1).
* **Qualifying names:** a name qualifies for the long leg at a rebalance if an opportunistic-purchase
  cluster *completed* (by filing date) within the 30 calendar days ending at t−1. It qualifies for the
  short leg if an opportunistic-sales cluster completed in the same window.
* **Contestant 1 (primary):** the equal-weight long portfolio of qualifying names against the
  **equal-weight union universe** (the comparator, rebalanced monthly on the same calendar). A month
  with no qualifying name holds the comparator (so the two series are paired on every session).
* **Contestant 2 (secondary, reported, not decisive):** long the purchase-cluster names, short the
  sales-cluster names, equal weight in each leg, both legs scaled to one unit of gross exposure; a
  missing leg is cash at the rate above.
* **Factor neutralization:** each contestant's monthly return is regressed on the market (SPY excess
  return), size (SMB), value (HML) and momentum (UMD) over the same window; the intercept series plus
  residuals is the neutralized return, reported beside the raw one. The factor returns are the
  published Fama–French/Carhart monthly factors as retrieved at run time and stored with the results;
  if a factor cannot be retrieved the run stops rather than substituting a proxy.
* **Costs:** the C1 cost model — 10 bps one-way (3 bps half-spread + 7 bps impact) on one-way turnover
  in NAV-weight space at every rebalance, charged identically to contestant and comparator, the
  initial position charged from all-cash, exactly as the C3 engine does. Short-leg borrow cost: not
  modeled, stated as a limitation (it can only hurt contestant 2, which is not decisive).

## E. Metrics, uncertainty and the pass threshold (fixed now)

* **Metrics** (after cost, on the window): annualized return; return per unit of volatility
  (annualized return ÷ annualized standard deviation of daily returns); maximum drawdown; mean annual
  one-way turnover; and the paired difference of each against the comparator.
* **Discriminating power, checked first:** the run reports the number of formation dates with at
  least one qualifying name and the mean and median number of qualifying names. If fewer than **36**
  formation dates have a qualifying name, or the median count is below **5**, the result is recorded
  as *inconclusive: insufficient breadth* and the candidate does not pass; a re-registration with a
  wider cluster definition would be a new candidate.
* **Uncertainty:** circular block bootstrap of the *daily net return series* of contestant and
  comparator jointly (the same block indices for both in every resample, preserving the pairing):
  block length 60 sessions, 1,000 resamples, seed **20260930**, each resample of length N; every
  metric except turnover recomputed on each resample; 90% intervals as the 5th–95th percentile. This
  is the statistic whose discriminating power the regime test v2 established and the options
  registration reuses.
* **Decision (contestant 1, neutralized series):** the candidate **passes only if** (i) the 90%
  interval of the paired difference in after-cost return per unit of volatility (contestant −
  comparator) lies entirely above zero, **and** (ii) the point estimate of the paired difference in
  annualized after-cost return is at least **+2.0 percentage points**, **and** (iii) the raw
  (un-neutralized) paired interval also lies above zero. Contestant 2 and the sensitivity in F are
  reported as context and do not enter the decision.
* **On a pass:** the candidate becomes eligible for a *separate* written decision on whether and how
  it enters a score on the union universe (never the book); the cluster flag's label changes from
  "candidate, not validated" to the test's result and date. **On a fail or an inconclusive result:**
  the flag stays descriptive, this document is restated in the results report with the mechanical
  diff (which must be empty), and nothing else changes.

## F. Sensitivities (reported, not decisive)

1. Insiders with fewer than three prior years of history **excluded** from classification (the paper's
   convention) instead of counted as opportunistic.
2. Cluster window 20 and 45 calendar days; minimum distinct buyers 2 and 4.
3. Officers and directors only (10% owners and "other" excluded).
4. Formation lag of one additional session after the filing date (t−2) as a check on the timing rule.
